**TL;DR:** For dishwasher leak water damage in San Marcos, shut off the water at the valve under the sink, then call an appliance repair technician to fix the dishwasher and a water damage restoration company, such as Flood Fixers at (855) 204-1124, to dry out the cabinet base and subfloor before it turns into mold or a warped floor. Dishwasher leaks are typically Category 2, gray water, under the IICRC S500 standard, because of detergent and food residue in the discharge, though a leak from the clean supply line before it reaches the appliance may start as Category 1, and either can degrade further the longer the water sits.

If you noticed a swollen toe-kick panel under the dishwasher, a soft spot in the tile or vinyl near the sink, or a musty smell coming from the cabinet below the counter, the leak has probably been running longer than you think. San Marcos kitchens built on slab foundations, from older ranch homes near Lake San Marcos to newer builds in the Creek District, often hide a slow dishwasher leak behind the cabinet toe-kick for weeks before anyone notices the floor flexing underfoot.

## Do You Call a Plumber, an Appliance Repair Tech, or a Restoration Company First?

Call whoever can stop the water first, an appliance repair technician or a plumber, then call a water damage restoration company the same day to dry out the cabinet base and subfloor before mold sets in.

The dishwasher itself, a cracked tub, a failed door gasket, a split supply line, or a loose drain hose, is a mechanical repair. An appliance technician or plumber isolates the failure and fixes or replaces the part. A restoration company does not repair appliances, and an appliance technician is not equipped to dry a wet subfloor.

Once the source is shut off, the water that already soaked into the particleboard cabinet base, the subfloor, or the drywall behind the toe-kick is a separate job. That's where a restoration company comes in: extracting any standing water, pulling wet cabinet material that can't be dried in place, and setting equipment to bring the subfloor back to a dry moisture reading before the cabinet box or flooring gets rebuilt. For the order of calls on other types of water loss around the house, see our [guide on who to call for water damage in San Marcos](/blog/who-to-call-for-water-damage-in-san-marcos/).

## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Company for a Dishwasher Leak?

A restoration technician measures moisture in the cabinet, subfloor, and surrounding walls with a moisture meter, removes any material that can't be dried in place, and runs drying equipment for several days until everything reads dry.

On the call, have ready: roughly when you first noticed the leak, whether the dishwasher is still running, and whether the floor near the dishwasher feels soft or looks discolored. That tells the technician how far the water likely traveled.

On arrival, the technician checks the cabinet base, the subfloor directly under the dishwasher, and the adjoining cabinets and wall framing, since water from a dishwasher commonly wicks sideways under cabinetry well past the appliance itself. If the toe-kick, cabinet floor, or particleboard base has already swollen or delaminated, it typically gets removed rather than dried in place. Air movers and a dehumidifier then run for 2 to 4 days, with daily moisture checks, until the subfloor and remaining cabinet materials test dry under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration.

## How Do You Know a Dishwasher Leak Has Already Caused Hidden Damage?

A musty smell from the cabinet, a soft or spongy spot in the flooring near the dishwasher, a warped or swollen cabinet base, or peeling laminate on the cabinet floor are all signs the leak has been running longer than a single incident.

A slow leak behaves differently than a burst line. It doesn't flood the kitchen; it seeps a little every wash cycle. Over weeks, that moisture works into the particleboard cabinet base, the subfloor underlayment, and sometimes the wall cavity behind the sink cabinet. Mold can start growing on wet material within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), so a leak caught the day it starts is a very different job than one that's been going for a month.

If you're smelling must or seeing discoloration and the leak has clearly been running a while, the job may need mold remediation in addition to drying. See our post on [how quickly mold grows after water damage](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/), and ask the restoration company to check for mold before they close out the drying, not after.

## Will Homeowners Insurance Cover a Dishwasher Leak in San Marcos?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a dishwasher, such as a supply line that fails without warning, but typically deny claims tied to a leak that was gradual or went unaddressed for an extended period.

That distinction, sudden versus gradual, is exactly why documentation matters. A restoration company's moisture readings and photos from the first visit become part of the record showing when the damage was caught and that drying started right away. For the general coverage pattern homeowners run into, see the [Insurance Information Institute's overview of what a standard homeowners policy covers](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance), and confirm the specifics with your own policy and adjuster.

Fixing the appliance and drying the area quickly isn't just about avoiding mold. It's also part of a homeowner's duty to mitigate further damage, which most policies require before they'll pay a claim.
