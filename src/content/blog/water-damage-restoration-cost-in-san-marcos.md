## How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in San Marcos?

Most San Marcos homeowners pay between $1,200 and $7,500 for the mitigation phase of a residential water loss, with whole-structure Category 3 losses running higher. That range covers extraction, structural drying, and antimicrobial treatment, but not repairs.

The spread is wide because "water damage" covers everything from a one-room dishwasher overflow to a slab leak that's been running under the foundation for days. Category (how contaminated the water is) and Class (how much material absorbed it) both move the price more than square footage alone. A small bathroom flood from a clean supply line costs far less to mitigate than the same square footage soaked by a sewage backup, because the second one requires more aggressive demolition and disposal under the [IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard.

## What Does Water Mitigation Cost Per Square Foot in San Marcos?

Mitigation in San Marcos typically runs $3.75 to $6.00 per square foot for Category 1 or 2 water, and $7.00 to $9.00 per square foot when Category 3 (black water) contamination requires more demolition and PPE.

That per-square-foot number covers extraction, air movers, dehumidification, and monitoring, not the equipment rental alone. If you're pricing equipment separately (for example, a DIY approach after a small leak), typical daily rental rates in the region run:

- Axial air mover: $35 to $50 per day
- Low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifier: $100 to $150 per day
- Air scrubber (for odor or mold-prone jobs): $75 to $125 per day

A standard drying setup for one affected room (three air movers, one dehumidifier) runs $205 to $300 per day in equipment alone, and most San Marcos jobs need three to five days of active drying to reach dry-standard moisture readings. That's before labor, monitoring visits, or antimicrobial application, which is why a packaged mitigation quote from a licensed crew almost always costs less than renting the same gear yourself and guessing at the drying timeline.

## What's the Water Damage Repair Cost After Mitigation?

Repair and rebuild costs are billed separately from mitigation, and for most San Marcos homes run $3,000 to $15,000 depending on finishes and how much material had to come out.

Mitigation stops the damage and dries the structure. It doesn't replace anything. Once a space is dry, you're looking at a second, separate scope: drywall, baseboard, flooring, and sometimes cabinetry or insulation.

| Scenario | Typical mitigation cost | Typical repair/rebuild cost |
|---|---|---|
| Single room, clean water (Cat 1) | $1,200 - $3,000 | $2,500 - $6,000 |
| Multi-room, gray water (Cat 2) | $3,000 - $7,500 | $6,000 - $15,000 |
| Whole-floor, black water (Cat 3) | $7,500 - $15,000+ | $12,000 - $30,000+ |
| Drywall replacement, per 100 sq ft | - | $350 - $900 |
| Hardwood floor replacement, per 200 sq ft | - | $2,400 - $5,600 |

Luxury finishes (engineered hardwood near Lake San Marcos, custom tile in a San Elijo Hills primary bath) push the top of that range higher. ## Flood Damage Restoration Cost vs. Water Damage Restoration Cost in San Marcos

Flood damage restoration typically costs more than standard water damage restoration because flood water is automatically treated as Category 3, requiring more demolition, disposal, and disinfection regardless of how clean the water looks.

"Water damage" usually means an indoor source: a burst pipe, failed appliance, or slow leak. "Flood damage" means water that entered from outside, groundwater, storm runoff, or a creek overflow, and it's presumed contaminated under IICRC guidelines even if it looks clear. That distinction isn't just semantic. It changes the PPE the crew wears, what materials get removed instead of dried in place (most flooded drywall and insulation below the waterline is removed, not saved), and the disposal costs. For a deeper comparison, see our [post on the difference between flood and water damage](/blog/difference-between-flood-damage-and-water-damage/). In practice, a flood-category loss in San Marcos runs 20 to 40 percent higher than a comparable clean-water loss of the same square footage, mostly from material replacement, not labor hours.

## Does Insurance Pay for Water Damage Restoration?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or failed water heater, but exclude gradual leaks and ground-sourced flooding unless you carry a separate flood policy.

That last part matters in San Marcos: standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood water from outside the home. That requires a National Flood Insurance Program policy or private flood rider. For the sudden-and-accidental losses that are covered, adjusters expect documentation: moisture readings, photos of the affected area before demolition, and an itemized drying log. Our [guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) breaks down what's typically covered line by line. A written scope with daily moisture logs is the single biggest factor in whether a claim gets approved without a fight.

## What Should You Do Next?

Start with a moisture assessment before signing anything, not an estimate guessed from a hallway walkthrough. The categorization (Cat 1, 2, or 3) and the moisture readings behind the walls are what actually set the price, and getting that wrong in either direction either underbids the job or overcharges you for work you don't need.
