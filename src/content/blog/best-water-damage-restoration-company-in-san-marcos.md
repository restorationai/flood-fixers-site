## What to Ask Before You Sign a Work Authorization

A work authorization is a binding contract, not a formality, and it's worth slowing down for even when water is actively spreading. Ask for these specifics before you sign anything:

- Proof of current IICRC certification for the technicians actually dispatched, not just the company name
- Whether the estimate covers mitigation only, or whether reconstruction is a separate contract with a separate company
- Whether the company bills your insurer directly or requires you to pay up front
- A written scope of work with square footage, equipment list, and an estimated drying timeline
- Cancellation terms and what happens if the final invoice exceeds the initial estimate

## Red Flags Worth Watching For

A few patterns show up consistently in bad outcomes and are worth screening for before you pick anyone. Pressure to sign a blank or vague authorization before the scope is explained is the biggest one. So is a crew that can't produce proof of certification on request, insists on cash payment only, or has no fixed local address tied to its listing. Reviews that are all dated within the same short window, or that read like templated marketing copy rather than specific accounts, deserve a second look too.
