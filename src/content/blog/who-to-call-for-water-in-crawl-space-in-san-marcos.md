## Do you call a plumber or a water damage company first?

Call whichever one can stop the water. If a supply line or fitting under the house is actively leaking right now, a plumber needs to isolate it before drying can start. If the source has already stopped, or the water came from rain and runoff rather than plumbing, a restoration crew should be your first call so extraction starts before mold gets a foothold.

The source also changes how the water gets treated. A sudden, clean supply-line failure is Category 1 water under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). Groundwater seeping in through the foundation under pressure is Category 2. Anything coming from a sewer line, a floor drain backup, or beyond a plumbing trap is Category 3, regardless of how it looks, and that changes what gets removed versus dried and what PPE the crew wears. If you're not sure which you're looking at, say so when you call; a restoration company trained to the S500 standard will categorize it on arrival rather than guess.

## Is water in a crawl space after heavy rain normal in San Marcos?

A little dampness or condensation in an unvented crawl space isn't unusual, but standing water after a storm is not something to shrug off. It almost always means grading, a clogged weep hole, a vent below grade, or a drainage gap is letting runoff in, and it needs to be pumped out and dried the same way a plumbing leak would be.

Repeated storm intrusion is also a sign the crawl space has a drainage problem that will keep recurring every rainy season until it's corrected, not just dried. ## What does a proper crawl space drying job actually include?

A proper crawl space drying job removes standing water, pulls insulation that can't be salvaged, dries the structural wood in place where possible, and confirms it's dry with a moisture meter, not a guess. Skipping any one of those steps is how homeowners end up with hidden mold six months later.

The typical sequence looks like this:

- **Water extraction.** Pumps and wet-vacs remove standing water from the crawl space floor, not just the visible puddles but the low points under vapor barrier if one is present.
- **Insulation removal.** Fiberglass batt insulation that's been saturated almost always comes out. Once it's compressed and wet it loses most of its R-value, and drying it in place rarely restores it. Rigid foam board sometimes survives and can be dried and reused.
- **Structural drying.** Joists, subfloor, and any exposed framing get dried in place with air movers and a dehumidifier sized to the space, run until moisture content drops to a safe range for the wood species. Severely delaminated or soft subfloor sections get cut out and replaced instead of dried.
- **Moisture verification.** Before and after readings with a penetrating or pin-type moisture meter document that the wood is actually dry, not just that it looks dry. This paperwork is also what an insurance adjuster wants to see.
