## Should I Call a Restoration Company or a Plumber First?

Call a plumber first only if the water source is still active and you can't stop it yourself; otherwise call a restoration company directly. If a supply line let go under a sink and you can reach the shutoff valve, turn it and then call for restoration. If you can't find a shutoff, or the leak is coming from a slab, a main line, or something underground, a plumber has to stop the flow before any drying equipment does much good.

Restoration work, extracting standing water, pulling wet flooring, drying saturated drywall, starts the moment the source is controlled. Waiting to call until a plumber has fully repaired the line can cost you a day you don't have. Mold can begin colonizing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, something the [EPA's mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold) spells out clearly, and San Diego's coastal humidity swings don't slow that clock down.

## When Do I Call 911 or the Utility Company Instead?

Call 911 or San Diego Gas & Electric before calling anyone else if water is near an active electrical panel, outlets are submerged, or you smell gas. Those are life-safety issues, not cleanup issues, and no restoration crew should enter a structure until the hazard is cleared.

Other situations that come before the restoration call: a ceiling that's visibly sagging or bowed (structural failure risk), a sewage backup that's reached a drinking water source, or standing water with visible sparking near an outlet. Once the scene is confirmed safe, restoration crews handle everything from there, including rooms affected by [Category 3 water](/blog/what-is-category-3-water-damage/) contamination.

## What Happens When You Call a Restoration Company?

The first call is a short triage conversation: what's the source, how long has water been standing, which rooms and floors are affected. That takes two or three minutes and determines how the crew dispatches.

On arrival, a technician typically works through these steps:

1. **Safety and source check.** Confirm the water source is controlled and the space is safe to work in.
2. **Moisture mapping.** Moisture meters and thermal imaging identify how far water has traveled behind walls and under flooring, following the [IICRC's](https://www.iicrc.org) S500 water damage restoration standard.
3. **Documentation.** Photos and moisture readings get logged for the insurance file, before anything is removed.
4. **Extraction.** Standing water comes out with truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment.
5. **Drying setup.** Air movers and dehumidifiers get positioned based on the moisture map, not guesswork.

Having your policy information, a few photos of the damage, and the location of your main water shutoff ready when you call speeds all of this up. If this is the first time you've dealt with a flooded space, [this walkthrough of the first 24 hours](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/) covers what to do while you wait.

## Do I Call My Insurance Company Before or After the Restoration Crew?

Call the restoration company first, then your insurer, because most homeowners policies require you to mitigate further damage right away, and a documented mitigation start helps a claim rather than hurting it. Delaying mitigation to "wait for approval" is one of the more common reasons claims get disputed.

A restoration crew's initial moisture readings, photos, and drying log become part of your claim file. That paper trail is usually stronger evidence of loss than anything you'd gather on your own after the fact.
