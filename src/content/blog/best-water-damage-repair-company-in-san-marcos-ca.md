## What Actually Matters When You Hire a Water Damage Company?

The right hire for a San Marcos home is the one that documents the loss the way your insurer needs it documented, not just the one that answers the phone fastest. Ask for proof of IICRC certification, ask whether the crew dries to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), and ask for a written scope before any demolition starts.

Also ask how the company categorizes your water. A burst supply line is usually Category 1 clean water when it starts, but it can degrade the longer it sits against drywall and flooring. Standing floodwater or a backed-up drain is a different category entirely and changes what has to be removed versus dried in place. A company that can explain this without hedging generally knows the work.

## Will My Homeowners Insurance Cover This?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, like a pipe that fails without warning, but they typically exclude gradual leaks and long-term seepage. Coverage details vary by policy and insurer, so this isn't a guarantee for your specific claim.

Documentation is what protects a borderline claim. Photos before anything is moved, a written drying log, and receipts for equipment and materials all matter more than most homeowners expect going in. For a deeper breakdown of what's typically covered and what isn't, see [our guide on homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## What Should You Do in the First Hour?

Stop the source of water if you can safely reach the shutoff valve, then start calling. If the water is still actively flowing from a burst pipe or failed appliance line, a plumber may need to come first to stop it before a restoration crew can begin drying. If the water has stopped and the damage is done, a restoration company is the right first call.
