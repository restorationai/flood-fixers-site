## What's the right order of calls when storm water is coming into your San Marcos home?

Call for safety first, extraction second, and your insurer third, in that order. Calling out of sequence either puts you at risk or weakens your claim.

If water has reached an outlet, a power strip, or your electrical panel, don't wade through it to reach the breaker. Call San Diego Gas & Electric or 911 if you can't shut off power without standing in water. Once the scene is electrically safe, call a water damage restoration company for extraction and drying, not your insurance company. Standing water does more damage every hour it sits, and most homeowners' policies require you to take reasonable steps to limit the loss. Photograph the water line and any visible entry point before extraction starts, then call your insurer once mitigation is underway so you can report what's already being done about it.

## Is storm water coming in under the door actually dangerous?

Yes. Treat any storm water that enters from outside as Category 3, also called black water, the most contaminated class defined by the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). It earns that classification because it has already crossed yards, driveways, and street gutters, picking up fertilizer, oil residue, and whatever else sits on the ground, before it ever reaches your floor.

That matters for two practical reasons. First, porous materials that sat in Category 3 water, baseboards, carpet pad, the bottom few inches of drywall, usually get removed rather than dried in place. Second, anyone working in it needs gloves and boots at minimum, and the [EPA's flooded-homes cleanup guidance](https://www.epa.gov/flooded-homes) is a reasonable starting point if you're doing any of the initial cleanup yourself before help arrives. ## What happens when you call for storm flood extraction?

A restoration crew arrives with pumps and extraction equipment, maps how far the water traveled, pulls the standing water, and sets drying equipment before leaving the first visit. Here's roughly how that visit breaks down:

1. **Phone triage.** You're asked where the water entered, how deep it is, and whether any electrical systems are affected, so the crew arrives with the right pumps and extraction gear already loaded.
2. **Extraction.** Submersible pumps clear standing water first; truck-mounted extractors pull the rest from carpet and flooring.
3. **Moisture mapping.** Moisture meters check behind baseboards and inside wall cavities for water that migrated past the visible waterline.
4. **Material decisions.** Anything porous that absorbed Category 3 water gets flagged for removal rather than drying in place.
5. **Drying setup.** Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in, and moisture readings get logged daily until the structure hits dry standard.

## Does homeowners insurance cover rain flooding in San Marcos?

Usually not on its own. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude rising surface water, which is exactly what happens when yard drains or hillside runoff overwhelm a lot during a storm, according to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/which-disasters-are-covered-by-homeowners-insurance). Covering that kind of loss generally requires a separate flood policy through the [National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood insurer](https://www.floodsmart.gov/).

That's different from a pipe bursting inside the house, which most standard policies do cover as a sudden, accidental loss.
