If water is actively spraying or pooling in your San Marcos home right now, stop reading after this paragraph and go shut off the water. Come back for the rest once the flow has stopped. A burst pipe is a two-call problem: one call to stop the water, one call to stop the damage. Here's exactly what to do, in order.

## What do you do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the water at the main shutoff valve before you do anything else. In most San Marcos homes built from the 1970s through 1990s, that valve sits near the street-side water meter or where the main line enters the garage or an exterior wall; turn it clockwise until it stops.

If water is pooling near an outlet, light switch, or your electrical panel, do not walk through it. Shut off power to that area at the breaker box if you can reach it without stepping in water. If you can't reach the panel safely, leave the house and call an electrician or the fire department before you go back in.

Once the water and any electrical risk are handled, grab your phone and take a few photos or a short video of the water on the floor, the wall, and anything it's touching. You'll want this for the insurance claim later, and it only takes a minute.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, close together: a licensed plumber stops the source, and a water damage restoration company stops the spread. Neither one replaces the other.

A plumber is the only one who can cut out and repair or replace the failed section of pipe. Until that's done, the leak isn't actually fixed, it's just shut off at the main. A restoration company handles everything the water already did: pulling standing water, drying out the walls and subfloor, and preventing the mold growth that can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall and framing, per the [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

Most homeowners in San Marcos, whether you're near Cal State San Marcos or over by Lake San Marcos, make the mistake of calling only the plumber and waiting on the water cleanup. That gap is exactly when mold gets a foothold inside walls and under flooring, which turns a one-day drying job into a demolition job. ## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

The first 24 hours are about getting water out and air moving, not rebuilding anything yet. A restoration crew starts with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps, then maps moisture in the walls, flooring, and subfloor with a moisture meter to find out how far the water actually traveled.

Based on that moisture map, they place air movers and a dehumidifier to dry the space to a dry standard, following the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). Water from a clean supply line usually starts as Category 1, but it degrades quickly once it sits in carpet padding or drywall for more than a few hours, so speed matters. Expect some baseboards pulled and a few inspection holes in drywall; that's normal and lets the crew confirm the wall cavity is actually drying, not just the surface.

For a fuller walkthrough of what that first day typically looks like room by room, see [our 24-hour water damage checklist](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/).

## Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in San Marcos?

Most standard homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, which a burst pipe usually is, but they typically exclude damage from gradual leaks, long-term seepage, or poor maintenance. If an adjuster finds evidence the pipe was leaking slowly for weeks before it finally gave way, that distinction can affect your claim.

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos, a list of damaged items, and receipts for any emergency mitigation work. The [Insurance Information Institute's guide to homeowners coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) is a good plain-language reference, and you have a duty to mitigate further damage once the leak is stopped, which is another reason drying can't wait. For a deeper breakdown of what's typically covered and what isn't, see [our guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/), and keep a copy of our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) on hand for next time.

Whichever plumber and restoration crew you choose, the sequence matters more than the names: stop the water, document it, then start drying within hours, not days.
