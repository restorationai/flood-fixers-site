## What commercial restoration actually involves

Commercial loss isn't just a bigger version of a residential job. A warehouse with exposed concrete floors dries differently than a medical office with carpeted exam rooms. A restaurant kitchen hit by a grease fire has soot bonded to stainless steel and HVAC ductwork that a retail boutique would never deal with. Commercial restoration means scoping each affected space separately, often room by room or suite by suite, and sequencing the work so some areas can reopen while others are still drying or being rebuilt. It also means dealing with property management, multiple stakeholders (owner, tenant, insurance carrier), and sometimes multiple insurance policies on the same building.

Equipment scales with square footage: commercial jobs often run multiple industrial air movers and dehumidifiers per zone, HEPA air scrubbers where indoor air quality affects employees or customers, and moisture meters to track drying progress across large open floor plans or drop-ceiling plenum spaces.

## Our process

1. **Pre-loss documentation and scope walkthrough.** Before anything is moved or dried, we photograph and document the affected areas, fixtures, and inventory, which becomes the basis for the insurance claim and the written scope of work.
2. **Source control and mitigation.** Water is extracted, fire-damaged debris is removed, or contaminated materials are isolated, depending on the loss type. The goal is to stop the damage from spreading to unaffected suites, floors, or shared systems like HVAC and electrical.
3. **Space-by-space drying or cleaning.** Office carpet, retail flooring, warehouse concrete, and commercial kitchen surfaces all require different drying times and cleaning agents. We monitor moisture and air quality by zone, not by building, so clean areas aren't held up by wet ones.
4. **Coordination with occupancy needs.** Where possible, we phase the work so non-affected areas can reopen for business while restoration continues elsewhere. This matters more for a retail storefront or clinic than it does for a vacant residential property.
5. **Reconstruction and compliance sign-off.** Drywall, flooring, and fixtures are rebuilt to match code, and where lead-safe work practices apply to older commercial buildings, that work follows EPA Lead-Safe protocols.

## What separates a good commercial restoration response from a bad one

The most common mistake on commercial jobs is treating the whole building as one drying zone instead of isolating affected suites. That slows down reopening and runs up equipment rental time unnecessarily. Another is skipping documentation of pre-existing conditions, like old water stains or prior fire damage, which adjusters will flag and use to dispute the claim if it isn't addressed upfront.

Adjusters reviewing commercial claims look closely at whether moisture readings were logged daily, whether contents and inventory loss were itemized separately from structural repair, and whether the drying equipment placement matches the documented wet area. A contractor who under-scopes a commercial loss to win the bid often leaves hidden moisture behind walls or under raised flooring, which shows up as mold or odor weeks later, after the business has already reopened.

## What does commercial restoration cost?

Commercial restoration costs vary widely based on square footage, business type, and how many trades are involved (electrical, HVAC, flooring, drywall). ## Seasonal & regional considerations

San Diego's older commercial corridors, think Hillcrest, North Park, and parts of downtown, still have buildings with original clay or cast-iron plumbing that fails without warning, often behind finished tenant improvements. Coastal humidity along the bay and in Mission Valley can slow structural drying in enclosed commercial spaces with limited ventilation, which is why moisture monitoring continues past the point where surfaces look dry. Flat commercial roofs common in industrial parks and strip retail centers are also more prone to slow leaks after the rare but heavy winter storm systems that move through the region.
