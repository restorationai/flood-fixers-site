Acidic soot keeps etching into metal, grout, and finished wood for days after a fire, and the smell embeds deeper into drywall and insulation with every hour of delay. If you're standing in a house that smells like a campfire and don't know what's safe to touch, that's the problem this service solves.

## What fire damage restoration actually involves

Fire damage repair covers everything between "the fire department just left" and "the house is livable again." That includes structural assessment of char and heat-weakened framing, soot and residue removal from every surface the smoke touched (which is almost always more surfaces than the visible burn area), odor neutralization, water extraction from fire suppression efforts, and coordination of repairs to framing, drywall, and finishes. A single-room kitchen fire can still leave smoke residue throughout an HVAC system and second-floor bedrooms, because smoke travels through ductwork and wall cavities long before anyone sees flame damage there. Timelines vary widely: a contained room fire with light smoke might clear in a week, while structural fire damage with charred framing and widespread soot can run several weeks once reconstruction is factored in.

## Our process

1. **Soot characterization.** Not all soot behaves the same. Protein soot from a kitchen grease fire is a thin, nearly invisible film that discolors and smells strong but wipes differently than the thick, oily synthetic soot left by burning plastics and furniture. We identify which type we're dealing with before choosing cleaning agents, because the wrong approach can smear soot deeper into porous materials.
2. **Structural and content triage.** We separate what can be cleaned, what needs specialized treatment, and what's a total loss, documenting each for the insurance file as we go.
3. **Thermal fogging and odor treatment.** Heat-based fogging opens the pores of surfaces so deodorizing agents can reach the same micro-level the smoke penetrated, rather than just masking the smell on top.
4. **Ozone or hydroxyl treatment.** For persistent smoke odor in structural cavities, controlled ozone or hydroxyl generator treatment breaks down odor-causing compounds that surface cleaning alone won't touch.
5. **Contents pack-out and cleaning.** Salvageable furniture, textiles, and belongings are inventoried, packed out, and cleaned off-site, then returned once the structure is ready.

## What separates a good fire damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating fire cleanup like a heavy-duty housecleaning job. Wiping visible soot off walls without addressing what's absorbed into porous drywall, carpet padding, or HVAC insulation leaves odor that resurfaces in humid weather months later. Another frequent miss: skipping a moisture check after fire suppression. Water from hoses or sprinklers sits behind baseboards and under flooring, and if it's not extracted and dried properly, you end up with a mold problem layered on top of a fire claim. Insurance adjusters pay close attention to documentation of pre-existing versus fire-caused damage, so a thorough photo and moisture-mapping record before any cleaning starts protects the claim as much as it protects the house. Good operators also test cleaning methods on inconspicuous areas first since high heat can change how a finish reacts to standard soot-removal agents.

## What does fire damage restoration cost?

Costs depend heavily on how far smoke traveled, whether structural framing was compromised, and how much needs to be replaced versus cleaned. Every loss is different, which is why Flood Fixers provides a written scope of work before any restoration begins.
