**Roof breached by wind-driven rain or a fallen tree limb?** Call now for emergency service before the next storm band moves through and turns a tarp job into a full interior rebuild.

A cracked tile, a torn section of shingles, or a cracked window seal doesn't always look urgent from the street. But once wind-driven rain finds that opening, water tracks along rafters and into insulation and drywall long before a stain shows up on the ceiling. San Diego's storm damage usually arrives in short, intense bursts, atmospheric river systems in winter and occasional tropical moisture in late summer, so the window between "roof is damaged" and "water is inside the walls" can be a matter of hours.

## What storm damage restoration actually involves

Storm damage restoration covers the structural and water-related fallout from wind, hail, heavy rain, and fallen trees: a punctured roof deck, a cracked window that let rain past the frame, a fence or carport crushed by a eucalyptus limb, or standing water pooling against a foundation because a downspout tore loose. The work usually has two phases. The first is stabilization, stopping further intrusion with tarping, board-up, and debris clearing. The second is the interior response, which looks a lot like water damage mitigation once moisture has entered framing, insulation, or flooring. Equipment includes tarping systems rated for wind exposure, structural fans and dehumidifiers for any wet cavities, and moisture meters to track how far water traveled past the point of entry.

## Our process

1. **Emergency stabilization.** Tarp the roof breach, board broken windows or doors, and remove or secure any hanging limbs or debris creating an ongoing hazard.
2. **Water intrusion response.** If rain got past the breach, we extract standing water and begin structural drying on affected framing, subfloor, and insulation before mold has a chance to establish.
3. **Debris and tree damage cleanup.** Fallen limbs, downed fencing, and storm debris are cleared from the structure and yard, with attention to anything still resting against the roofline or siding.
4. **Damage documentation and moisture mapping.** We photograph wind and water damage separately, log moisture readings at the point of intrusion and surrounding materials, and build a record adjusters can work from.
5. **Reconstruction scope.** For torn roofing, damaged siding, or compromised framing, we put together a written scope of repair work before anything is rebuilt.

## What separates a good storm damage response from a bad one

The biggest mistake is treating storm damage as purely cosmetic. Hail and wind can compromise a roof's underlayment without breaking the surface shingles, which means a roof can leak during the next rain even though it looked fine right after the storm. A rushed tarp job that doesn't account for wind uplift often fails within days, letting more water in than the original breach did. Adjusters are looking for clear separation between wind damage (torn shingles, cracked siding, broken glass) and water damage that followed it, along with moisture readings that show how far intrusion traveled. Operators who skip that documentation, or who dry the surface without checking subfloor and wall cavities, tend to leave hidden moisture behind that surfaces as mold weeks later.

## What does storm damage restoration cost?

Costs vary widely depending on whether the loss is limited to exterior stabilization or involves interior water intrusion and structural repair. Every storm event is different, and Flood Fixers provides a written scope of work before any repair begins so there are no surprises once the roof is opened up. Most homeowners insurance policies cover sudden storm damage, wind, and falling-tree events under standard dwelling coverage, though flood-specific water intrusion may require separate coverage, which is worth confirming with your agent.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

San Diego's storm risk is concentrated in winter atmospheric river events, when sustained rain and gusty wind arrive together and expose roofs that held up fine through a dry summer. Santa Ana wind events, though typically dry, can also tear loose aging roofing and drop eucalyptus and pine limbs common in older coastal and canyon neighborhoods. Hurricane and tornado activity is rare for the region, but the same wind-driven rain mechanics that cause hurricane damage elsewhere apply here on a smaller scale, which is why roof age and flashing condition matter more than storm severity alone.
