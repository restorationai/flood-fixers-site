## Why Camp Pendleton South Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Camp Pendleton South sits in the coastal stretch of northern San Diego County, where Pacific storm systems and periodic El Niño winters bring short, intense rain events onto ground that's dry and compacted most of the year. That combination means storm drains and swales built for a typical dry season can get overwhelmed quickly when a heavier system moves through. Much of the housing stock here was built as part of military family housing development, with slab foundations and shared grading across rows of homes, so when one low point floods, the water often tracks along several addresses rather than staying contained to a single yard or unit. Add in base-adjacent infrastructure, shared utility corridors, and the coastal humidity that slows natural drying, and a flood event here behaves differently than the same storm would in an inland San Diego neighborhood.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Camp Pendleton South

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted and portable pumps depending on how the property is accessed. Next we assess what's underneath: slab foundations hide moisture differently than raised construction, so we use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find water that's migrated under flooring or into wall cavities before it becomes a mold problem. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space and monitored daily until materials read dry, not just dry to the touch. Because coastal air carries more ambient humidity than inland San Diego, we often run equipment a day or two longer here to get a true dry reading before reconstruction starts.
