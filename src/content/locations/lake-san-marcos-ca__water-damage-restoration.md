## Why Lake San Marcos Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lake San Marcos was developed as a resort and retirement community built around its namesake lake, and a lot of the housing stock near the water dates to that original build-out. Homes close to the shoreline often sit on slab foundations with a naturally higher water table underneath them, which means a failed water heater or supply line doesn't just create a puddle, it pushes moisture sideways under tile and laminate before anyone notices a problem. Mature landscaping and golf-course-adjacent irrigation systems add another variable: slow leaks from irrigation lines or drip systems can mimic a plumbing leak, and figuring out which one you're dealing with changes the whole mitigation plan. San Diego County's dry climate for most of the year can also work against homeowners, because a slow leak that would announce itself with visible mold in a humid region can sit quietly in a wall cavity here for weeks before anyone smells it.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lake San Marcos

We start with extraction, pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad before it migrates further into subfloor or slab. From there we inspect for the kind of hidden saturation common in homes near the lake, checking baseboards, cabinet toe-kicks, and the underside of flooring where water tends to travel instead of pool. Drying is calibrated to the materials involved: a water-intruded slab with tile over it dries on a different timeline than drywall and insulation in a stick-built wall, and we set air movers and dehumidifiers accordingly rather than running a one-size-fits-all setup. We track moisture content daily with meters and document readings so the drying log lines up with what your adjuster expects to see when the claim is reviewed.
